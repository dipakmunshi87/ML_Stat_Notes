The honest summary: one of the four is load-bearing at any size, and the other three only start to matter with depth.

The block

What it looks like

Two sublayers. Attention mixes information between the objects; a small network then acts on each object separately. Both are added back onto a running total rather than replacing it.

You have seen this before

Interaction, then local evolution, applied alternately — an operator-splitting step, exactly as in a split-step scheme for a nonlinear wave equation. Depth is the number of steps.

The split-step reading also explains why stacking works: it is a first-order integrator for a flow.

The division of labour

Attention: which objects talk to which.
The small network: what each object does with what it heard.
Nothing else moves information between objects, so every long-range effect in the model passes through an attention matrix.

The residual, as physics

Xℓ+1=Xℓ+F(Xℓ)X_{\ell+1}=X_\ell+F(X_\ell)Xℓ+1​=Xℓ​+F(Xℓ​) is a forward Euler step of X˙=F(X)\dot X=F(X)X˙=F(X). A deep stack is an integrator with a learned right-hand side, and depth behaves like time. It is also why very deep stacks train at all: the gradient travels back along the identity path.

Pre-norm versus post-norm is not cosmetic: post-norm needs learning-rate warm-up at depth, pre-norm largely does not.

Several at once

Why more than one kernel

One attention matrix imposes one notion of relevance on the whole layer. In physics terms, one interaction. Real systems usually have several, so transformers run hhh of them side by side on subspaces of width dk=d/hd_k=d/hdk​=d/h and add the results:

MHA(X)=[ head1,…,headh ]WO.\mathrm{MHA}(X)=\big[\,\mathrm{head}_1,\dots,\mathrm{head}_h\,\big]W_O .MHA(X)=[head1​,…,headh​]WO​.

These are called heads. They are free: hhh heads of width d/hd/hd/h is the same arithmetic as one head of width ddd. In a trained model one head tends to track near neighbours, another a global average, another some specific kind of partner.

Two variants you will meet. Masking forbids a token from seeing later ones — that is what makes a model generative, and it is page 6. Cross-attention takes the queries from one set and the keys and values from another, which is how a model is conditioned on an image, a spectrum or a set of parameters.

Physics picture

Channels in a Hamiltonian. A short-range term, a long-range term, a species-selective term — each is one head, and they are summed at the end. A single head can only express one of them.

Typical numbers

d=768d=768d=768, h=12h=12h=12, dk=64d_k=64dk​=64 was the original BERT-base setting and is still a common choice. Large modern models use 16 to 128 heads.

On reading heads

A head that looks like it tracks a physical neighbour may be doing something else. The honest test is to switch it off and measure what changes.

Grouped-query attention — sharing K and V across heads — is an inference-memory optimisation, not an accuracy one.

Position

The one thing the sum cannot see

Look back at the N-body sum. Nothing in it refers to the order of the particles — and nothing in attention does either. Shuffle the rows and the outputs shuffle with them, exactly. The operation sees a set.

When order is physical, you have to put it in by hand. Three ways are in use:

  • Sinusoidal. Add sin⁡(t/λk)\sin(t/\lambda_k)sin(t/λk​) and cos⁡(t/λk)\cos(t/\lambda_k)cos(t/λk​) over a ladder of wavelengths — Fourier features of the index. No parameters, and it extends to lengths never seen in training.
  • Learned. A free vector per position. Simple; cannot extend past the longest training sequence.
  • Rotary. Rotate qqq and kkk by an angle proportional to position, so that qi ⁣⋅ ⁣kjq_i\!\cdot\!k_jqi​⋅kj​ depends only on i−ji-ji−j. Relative position, built into the score. What most current models use.
The useful version for physics

Do not encode a row number. Encode the coordinate. Feed Fourier features of a particle position, a wavelength, a redshift or a time stamp, and the model gets the real geometry instead of an index.

This is the single most useful adaptation of a transformer to physical data, and it is why unevenly sampled measurements need no special handling: there is no grid for them to be irregular against.

Which symmetry do you want?

A positional encoding is a choice of geometry. Rotary says "a line, with translation invariance". For a point cloud you usually want no ordering at all — so add nothing, and keep the symmetry you already have.

Replacing the index with a coordinate is the practical recommendation of this whole page.

The scaling

Where 1/dk1/\sqrt{d_k}1/dk​​ comes from

That divisor in the softmax looks arbitrary. It is a variance estimate, and it is the one piece of the block that is genuinely load-bearing.

At the start of training, qqq and kkk have independent components of unit variance. Their dot product is a sum of dkd_kdk​ independent terms, so

Var⁡(q ⁣⋅ ⁣k)=dk,spread=dk.\operatorname{Var}(q\!\cdot\!k)=d_k,\qquad\text{spread}=\sqrt{d_k}.Var(q⋅k)=dk​,spread=dk​​.

At dk=64d_k=64dk​=64 the scores are spread over ±8\pm8±8. Put that into a softmax and you get near one-hot weights before any learning has happened: measured over six tokens, the largest weight is 0.94 without the scaling and 0.49 with it.

A saturated softmax has almost no gradient — $\partial A_{ij}/\partial s_{ik}=A_{ij}(\delta_{jk} -A_{ik})\to0when when whenA$ is one-hot — so the model is frozen at a random configuration and cannot move. Dividing by dk\sqrt{d_k}dk​​ starts it hot, where it can still respond.

In the temperature language

Without the scaling the system starts frozen, and a frozen system has no susceptibility. The 1/dk1/\sqrt{d_k}1/dk​​ sets the initial temperature high enough that the network can choose how far to cool.

The general principle

Every constant in a deep architecture — this one, the 1/fan-in\sqrt{1/\text{fan-in}}1/fan-in​ of weight initialisation, layer normalisation itself — is there to keep signals at unit variance. Variance bookkeeping, not magic numbers.

And it depends on dkd_kdk​

Remove it at dk=8d_k=8dk​=8 and nothing happens. Remove it at dk=64d_k=64dk​=64 and the model fails completely. The next slide races exactly that.

The vanishing-gradient argument is the real content; "it keeps the variance at one" is the symptom.

Read rows two and five together

Removing the scaling at dk=64d_k=64dk​=64 does not slow the model down — it stops it. The error is worse than guessing, and the attention is at chance. The identical change at dk=8d_k=8dk​=8 is invisible. The code is the same; only dk\sqrt{d_k}dk​​ differs.

And rows three and four

Residual connections and normalisation cost nothing here. That is not because they are useless: it is because this model is one layer deep and they are insurance against depth. An ablation at one layer tells you nothing about twenty-four, in either direction — a trap worth remembering when reading papers.

This is also a warning about small-scale ablation studies, including in physics applications.

If you remember one thing

The block alternates mixing between objects with mixing within an object, inside an identity path. Everything else is there to keep that alternation numerically well behaved as you stack it up.

An experiment that did not work

I tried to show that a network trained on masses coupled by springs would recover the coupling matrix in its attention. It did — on two random starts out of three. By the standard of page 7 that is not a result, so it is a drawing on page 3 rather than a demonstration here.

The failed experiment is included deliberately: a set that only shows what worked is teaching the wrong habit.
