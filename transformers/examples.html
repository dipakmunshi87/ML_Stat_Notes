How it is set up

A jet, turned into a set of tokens

Each particle becomes one row. The columns are whatever you choose to tell the network about it — momentum, position relative to the jet axis, and any pairwise quantity you think matters.

One extra row carries no kinematics and asks the question. The answer is read out of that row after the network has let it listen to all the others.

The "question token" is usually called a class token; the name does not help anybody.

Particle physics

Jets and tracks

Jet tagging — the clearest case

A jet is a set of constituents with no natural order and a size that changes event to event: exactly the data type attention was built for. Particle Transformer (Qu, Li & Qian, ICML 2022) came with JetClass — 100 million jets in 10 classes, a hundred times larger than anything public before it — and beat the previous best, ParticleNet, by a clear margin.

The detail worth copying: it feeds pairwise physics quantities — ΔR\Delta RΔR, kTk_TkT​, pair mass — straight into the scores. The interaction is supplied, not learned.

Tracking

Which hits belong to the same particle? TrackFormers (EPJ C 2025) treats hits as tokens and lets attention decide the grouping, aimed at the high-luminosity LHC where classical combinatorial tracking scales badly with pile-up.

Why this field first

Three reasons. The data really is a set; simulation gives effectively unlimited labelled examples; and the tasks are narrow enough to benchmark honestly. Few other fields have all three.

What limits it

All-pairs over thousands of hits is why tracking work uses windowed or hierarchical attention rather than the full matrix. At detector scale the cost is the design constraint, not a footnote.

The pattern

The winning models are not plain transformers. They are transformers with physics pushed into the scores: pairwise kinematics, locality in ΔR\Delta RΔR, symmetry-respecting features.

The pairwise-feature trick is the single most transferable idea on this slide.

Astronomy

Uneven data, and several kinds at once

Photometric transients

Allam & McEwen, RASTI 3, 209 (2024), applied a transformer to the PLAsTiCC simulated LSST dataset: ROC AUC 0.98 and PR AUC 0.87, comparable to the best hand-built classifiers with far less feature work.

The structure matters more than the number. As tokens (t,f,σf,band)(t,f,\sigma_f,\text{band})(t,f,σf​,band), uneven multi-band sampling is simply not a difficulty; on a grid it needs interpolation you then have to defend.

Images and spectra together

AstroCLIP (Parker, Lanusse, Golkar et al., MNRAS 531, 4990 (2024)) puts DESI spectra and Legacy Survey images into one shared space. Without any task-specific training: photometric redshifts as good as a supervised ResNet18, and physical properties better than the supervised baseline by 19% in R2R^2R2.

Why that direction is interesting

A survey gives several views of the same object — image, spectrum, light curve. Lining them up in one representation turns unlabelled survey data into a training signal.

Read that result carefully

"As good as a supervised baseline without task-specific training" is a statement about the quality of the representation. It is a real result. It is not the same as a better photometric redshift.

The 19% gain is on physical properties, where the spectrum carries information the image does not — which is the honest explanation of where it comes from.

Elsewhere

Three more worth knowing

Weather

Pangu-Weather (Bi et al., Nature 619, 533 (2023)) runs a transformer on a 0.25° global grid — the same grid as the ECMWF operational model — and reported better RMSE than it on geopotential, humidity, wind and temperature, from one hour to one week. The first time a learned model beat operational forecasting on its own metrics.

Quantum many-body

The amplitude of a spin or fermion configuration produced by a transformer, optimised variationally. Attention supplies the long-range correlations a local ansatz has to work for, and sampling gives exact independent configurations with no Markov chain. Active, and genuinely competitive.

Molecules and materials

Attention appears throughout, but usually in equivariant form on a neighbourhood graph rather than plain. The lesson generalises: the successful versions are the ones carrying the symmetry, not the ones with the most parameters.

A caution that applies to all three

These models are judged against the distribution they were trained on. Skill on ordinary conditions does not imply skill on the extreme events that motivate the forecast in the first place, and showing the latter takes a deliberate test rather than an average score.

The weather result is the one most often over-generalised: RMSE-optimal models are systematically over-smoothed.

Interactive

One prong or two, decided live

A toy jet is a spray of particles in the (Δη,Δϕ)(\Delta\eta,\Delta\phi)(Δη,Δϕ) plane. Did they come from one prong or two? No single particle can tell you. Two networks train at once: one is handed ΔR2\Delta R^2ΔR2 for each particle, the other is not.

The lesson

One good feature beats three times the model

Identical seeds and examples throughout. Guessing gets 50%.

One extra number per particle — one the network could in principle work out for itself from coordinates it already has — is worth more than tripling it or doubling the training.

Why that feature helps so much

Attention takes averages. It does not naturally form second moments. "One cluster or two" is a question about spread, and handing it ΔR2\Delta R^2ΔR2 gives it a quantity whose average is a spread. Without it, the same answer needs several layers to build up.

This is what ParT does

Its pairwise features — ΔR\Delta RΔR, kTk_TkT​, pair mass — are the same move at production scale: supply the physically meaningful quantity rather than hoping the network rebuilds it. The largest single gain in jet tagging came from this, not from more parameters.

The general form

Before making a model bigger, ask what physical quantity it is being forced to rediscover, and give it that instead. It is almost always cheaper.

This slide is the practical heart of the set: effort belongs in the representation, not the parameter count.

What the successes have in common

Every entry in the top half has plenty of labelled data — from simulation or from a survey — and an input that is genuinely a set. Where either is missing the results thin out quickly, and that is the honest predictor of whether it will work on your problem.
