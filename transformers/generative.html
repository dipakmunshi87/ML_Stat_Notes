The whole idea

One triangle, and a Markov chain

The chain-rule factorisation is exact, which is what separates this family from generative models that only approximate a distribution.

The factorisation

Chain rule, enforced by a mask

Any joint distribution factorises exactly:

p(x1,…,xL)=∏i=1Lp(xi ∣ x<i).p(x_1,\dots,x_L)=\prod_{i=1}^{L}p(x_i\,|\,x_{<i}).p(x1​,…,xL​)=i=1∏L​p(xi​∣x<i​).

That is the chain rule, not an approximation. So a model that produces p(xi∣x<i)p(x_i|x_{<i})p(xi​∣x<i​) at every site defines a proper normalised distribution over whole configurations. The only requirement is that site iii must not see the sites after it, which one line in the softmax enforces:

Aij=0  for j>i.A_{ij}=0\ \text{ for } j>i .Aij​=0  for j>i.

One line in the softmax, and the network is a generative model. Training is unchanged — predict the next value, minimise the negative log-likelihood — and the loss now has an exact meaning: the cross-entropy between data and model, in nats per site.

Verified here: changing the last input leaves every earlier output identical to 0 in double precision, and the gradient through the masked path matches finite differences to 9×10−7.

Two things this buys

An exact probability. You can compute the probability of any configuration in one pass. For physics that is the difference between a picture generator and a usable statistical model.

Independent samples. Generation draws site by site with no Markov chain — no burn-in, no autocorrelation time, no critical slowing down.

The honest qualification

Those samples are independent draws from the model, not from the true distribution. The fix is standard and should always be used: treat the model as a proposal in an independence Metropolis step, or reweight by e−βE/pθe^{-\beta E}/p_\thetae−βE/pθ​. Then the answer is exact and the network only affects the efficiency.

The reweighting point is what makes this respectable as physics rather than as approximation: the model can be wrong and the final estimator still unbiased.

The physics reading

A learned transfer matrix

Take the 1D Ising chain, E=−∑isisi+1E=-\sum_i s_is_{i+1}E=−∑i​si​si+1​, at inverse temperature β\betaβ. Its conditional distribution is exactly Markov:

p(si ∣ s<i)=p(si ∣ si−1),p(si=si−1)=1+tanh⁡β2,p(s_i\,|\,s_{<i})=p(s_i\,|\,s_{i-1}),\qquad p(s_i=s_{i-1})=\frac{1+\tanh\beta}{2},p(si​∣s<i​)=p(si​∣si−1​),p(si​=si−1​)=21+tanhβ​,

which is the transfer matrix T=eβσz⊗σzT=e^{\beta\sigma_z\otimes\sigma_z}T=eβσz​⊗σz​ written as a conditional. An autoregressive model over spins is therefore a learned transfer matrix — and in 1D it can represent the truth exactly, so we know what it should converge to.

That gives a rare thing: a generative model with an analytic answer to check against. The correlation function of the exact chain is

⟨sisi+k⟩=tanh⁡kβ,\langle s_i s_{i+k}\rangle=\tanh^k\beta,⟨si​si+k​⟩=tanhkβ,

and the next slide measures it on configurations the network invented.

Why this generalises

In two dimensions the conditional p(si∣s<i)p(s_i|s_{<i})p(si​∣s<i​) is no longer Markov — it depends on a whole boundary — and that is precisely where attention earns its place: it can attend to the relevant part of the boundary at any distance. This is the basis of autoregressive neural samplers for spin systems and lattice field theories.

The variational route

You do not even need data. Minimising the variational free energy Fθ=Epθ[E+β−1log⁡pθ]F_\theta=\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta}[E+\beta^{-1}\log p_\theta]Fθ​=Epθ​​[E+β−1logpθ​] trains the sampler directly against the Hamiltonian, using its own samples. That is a physics training objective with no dataset at all, and it gives a variational bound on the free energy as a by-product.

The known difficulty

Trained this way, models tend to mode-collapse onto one ordered phase near a first-order transition. It is a real and active problem, not a solved one.

The variational free-energy objective is the cleanest example anywhere of a machine learning method whose loss function is a physical quantity.

What this shows, and what it does not

The model reproduces a correlation function it was never given, from configurations it invented, to within a few per cent. It was shown nothing but sampled chains — no energy, no β\betaβ, no formula.

It is also systematically over-correlated at every kkk. That bias is small but real, and it is exactly the sort of thing that would propagate into a physical estimate unnoticed.

Which is why you reweight

With the energy function in hand, importance weights e−βE/pθe^{-\beta E}/p_\thetae−βE/pθ​ remove that bias entirely and turn the residual mismatch into a loss of efficiency rather than an error in the answer. The effective sample size then tells you how good the model is — an honest, quantitative diagnostic that a generated picture never gives you.

The over-correlation is the honest headline: a generative model that looks right can be biased at the few-per-cent level, which is large compared with modern statistical errors.

The other families

Where transformers sit in modern generative models

Diffusion

Corrupt data with a forward SDE until it is noise, then learn to run it backwards. The network's job is to predict the score ∇xlog⁡pt(x)\nabla_x\log p_t(x)∇x​logpt​(x), and in current models that network is a transformer operating on patches (a "DiT") rather than a convolutional U-Net. Attention is the backbone; the generative mathematics is stochastic differential equations.

Flows

An invertible map from a simple distribution to the target, with a tractable Jacobian — so again an exact likelihood. Used in lattice field theory to sample gauge configurations with the correct measure, with the model as a proposal and an accept step restoring exactness. Flow matching trains the same object by regression against a velocity field.

Autoregressive

The family on this page. Exact likelihood, independent samples, natural for discrete degrees of freedom — spins, occupation numbers, particle lists. Generation costs LLL forward passes, which is the main practical drawback.

The companion set

The stochastic-differential-equation document works through the diffusion-model connection properly: Anderson's reverse-time SDE, the variance-preserving and variance-exploding forms, and the probability-flow ODE. Everything in the first card is derived there.

Worth stressing that "diffusion model" names the generative process, not the architecture — the architecture inside is usually a transformer.

In physics practice

Four uses, honestly rated

Fast event and detector simulation — working

Calorimeter showers and full events generated in milliseconds instead of seconds of Geant4. The physics question is not visual quality but whether the generated distributions match in the tails that the analysis actually uses; the community benchmarks now test exactly that.

Lattice configurations — promising

Flow-based samplers for gauge theories, aimed at critical slowing down. Exact when combined with an accept step. Scaling to physically interesting volumes is the open question, and the honest current answer is "not yet, but the trend is good".

Simulation-based inference — working

When the likelihood is intractable but simulation is possible, learn the posterior, the likelihood or a ratio directly from simulated pairs. A set-valued observation is a natural transformer input. The discipline is coverage testing: a learned posterior must be shown to be calibrated, not assumed to be.

Language models for physics — mixed

Genuinely useful for code, literature search and algebraic manipulation, where output can be checked. Not a source of physical truth: a fluent derivation is not a correct one, and the failure mode is confident plausibility. Use them where verification is cheap, and verify.

The common thread: every legitimate use has a cheap external check — an accept step, a coverage test, a compiler, a referee.

The one-sentence version

A generative model of a physical system is worth having when it comes with a way to check it — an exact likelihood, an accept step, a conserved quantity, a known correlation function. Without one it produces configurations that look right, and looking right is not a measurement.
