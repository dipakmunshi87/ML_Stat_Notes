Method 2 of 5 · one coordinate at a time

Gibbs sampling

Instead of proposing a move for all the parameters at once, update them one at a time, each drawn from its own exact conditional distribution. Nothing is ever rejected. The price is that every move is parallel to an axis.

Gibbs sampling is the workhorse behind BUGS, JAGS and much of applied Bayesian statistics from the 1990s. It is still the right tool whenever the conditionals are standard distributions you can sample directly.

The algorithm

Sweep through the conditionals

For parameters θ=(θ1,…,θd)\boldsymbol\theta=(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_d)θ=(θ1​,…,θd​), one sweep is:

θ1(t+1)∼π(θ1∣θ2(t),…,θd(t))θ2(t+1)∼π(θ2∣θ1(t+1),θ3(t),…)    ⋮θd(t+1)∼π(θd∣θ1(t+1),…,θd−1(t+1))\begin{aligned} \theta_1^{(t+1)}&\sim\pi\big(\theta_1\mid\theta_2^{(t)},\dots,\theta_d^{(t)}\big)\\ \theta_2^{(t+1)}&\sim\pi\big(\theta_2\mid\theta_1^{(t+1)},\theta_3^{(t)},\dots\big)\\ &\;\;\vdots\\ \theta_d^{(t+1)}&\sim\pi\big(\theta_d\mid\theta_1^{(t+1)},\dots,\theta_{d-1}^{(t+1)}\big) \end{aligned}θ1(t+1)​θ2(t+1)​θd(t+1)​​∼π(θ1​∣θ2(t)​,…,θd(t)​)∼π(θ2​∣θ1(t+1)​,θ3(t)​,…)⋮∼π(θd​∣θ1(t+1)​,…,θd−1(t+1)​)​

Each line uses the most recent value of every other coordinate. The conditional is a one-dimensional density, proportional to the full posterior with the other coordinates held fixed:

π(θi∣θ−i)=π(θ)∫π(θ) dθi  ∝  π(θ)∣θ−i fixed.\pi(\theta_i\mid\boldsymbol\theta_{-i})=\frac{\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)}{\int\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)\,d\theta_i}\;\propto\;\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)\Big|_{\boldsymbol\theta_{-i}\ \rm fixed}.π(θi​∣θ−i​)=∫π(θ)dθi​π(θ)​∝π(θ)​θ−i​ fixed​.
Why nothing is rejected

Each update draws from the exact conditional, so it leaves π\piπ unchanged: if θ∼π\boldsymbol\theta\sim\piθ∼π before the update, it is still distributed as π\piπ afterwards. It is a Metropolis–Hastings step whose acceptance probability is exactly 1.

The catch

You must be able to draw from each conditional. That is easy for conjugate models (Gaussian, gamma, Dirichlet, …) and hard otherwise, which is where Gibbs-within-Metropolis comes in.

Physics picture

This is heat-bath Monte Carlo: put one spin in contact with the bath and resample it from its conditional given its neighbours. Geman & Geman introduced it for exactly that, image models on a lattice.

A sweep of ddd conditionals costs roughly the same as one ddd-dimensional density evaluation, so comparisons per "iteration" should be made carefully.

Each move is horizontal or vertical, which is what produces the staircase.

At ρ = 0 the samples are independent and the efficiency is 100%. Push ρ to 0.98 and the chain climbs the diagonal one small tread at a time. Theory says the lag-1 autocorrelation of a full sweep is exactly ρ2\rho^2ρ2, so the efficiency falls as (1−ρ2)/(1+ρ2)(1-\rho^2)/(1+\rho^2)(1−ρ2)/(1+ρ2); the readout shows the measured value beside that prediction.

The geometry

Axis-aligned moves, and what they cost

For the bivariate Gaussian above, the conditional mean pulls only a fraction ρ\rhoρ of the way:

θ1∣θ2∼N(ρ θ2,  1−ρ2).\theta_1\mid\theta_2\sim\mathcal N\big(\rho\,\theta_2,\;1-\rho^2\big).θ1​∣θ2​∼N(ρθ2​,1−ρ2).

A full sweep therefore contracts the deviation from the mean by ρ2\rho^2ρ2, and

r1=ρ2,τ=1+ρ21−ρ2,Neff=Nτ.r_1=\rho^2,\qquad \tau=\frac{1+\rho^2}{1-\rho^2},\qquad N_{\rm eff}=\frac{N}{\tau}.r1​=ρ2,τ=1−ρ21+ρ2​,Neff​=τN​.

At ρ=0.99\rho=0.99ρ=0.99, τ≈100\tau\approx100τ≈100: a hundred sweeps per independent sample. The picture is the same as the random walk on a ridge: the sampler can only move along the axes, while the probability mass lies along a diagonal.

The failure mode

Strong correlation between parameters. It is not a bug in the sampler but a mismatch between the coordinates you chose and the shape of the posterior.

Three cures
  • Reparameterise: sample the sum and difference instead of the two correlated quantities.
  • Block: update correlated parameters jointly from their joint conditional, when that is available.
  • Collapse: integrate out a nuisance parameter analytically (Rao–Blackwellisation), which usually reduces correlation as well as variance.
Physics picture

Critical slowing down: as correlations grow, local single-site updates need more and more sweeps to change anything global. Cluster algorithms (Swendsen–Wang) were invented to fix exactly this in spin models.

The blocking idea is the important one: Gibbs on blocks that are internally correlated but weakly correlated with each other is often the best sampler available.

A worked example

Where the conditionals write themselves

Why this matters

No tuning, no step size, no acceptance rate, and it scales to thousands of parameters μj\mu_jμj​. This is why Gibbs dominated applied Bayesian work for two decades, and why it remains the method of choice for conditionally conjugate hierarchical models and for models with discrete latent labels (mixtures, changepoints, image segmentation).

The weak spot of this very model is the funnel: when τ\tauτ is small the μj\mu_jμj​ are squeezed together, and Gibbs mixes badly. Non-centred parameterisation, μj=m+τηj\mu_j=m+\tau\eta_jμj​=m+τηj​ with ηj∼N(0,1)\eta_j\sim\mathcal N(0,1)ηj​∼N(0,1), is the standard fix and helps HMC too.

When conditionals are not standard

Gibbs within Metropolis

If one conditional cannot be sampled directly, replace that draw with any update that leaves the conditional invariant:

  • a one-dimensional Metropolis step (Metropolis-within-Gibbs);
  • a slice sampling step, which needs no tuning;
  • adaptive rejection sampling, when the conditional is log-concave.

The composition of updates, each leaving π\piπ invariant, still leaves π\piπ invariant. This is the general principle that makes MCMC modular.

Careful

Invariance composes; detailed balance does not have to hold for each piece of a sweep. A deterministic sweep order is fine, but it breaks reversibility of the whole sweep — harmless in practice, and avoided entirely by choosing the update order at random.

The modern view

Gibbs is one special case of a bigger family: pick a subset of coordinates, apply any π\piπ-invariant kernel, repeat. Modern probabilistic programming systems (Stan, NumPyro, PyMC) mostly use NUTS on the continuous parameters and Gibbs-style updates for the discrete ones.

Physics picture

Exactly the heat-bath versus Metropolis choice in lattice simulations: heat bath where the local distribution can be sampled directly, Metropolis where it cannot.

Slice-within-Gibbs is a very robust default: no tuning parameters, no acceptance rate, and it adapts its own scale to each conditional.

Read the small print

This mixture is axis-aligned: the four modes sit at the corners of a square aligned with the coordinates, so each conditional is itself an exact two-component mixture, and a single Gibbs step can jump between modes. Rotate the target by 45° and Gibbs would fail as badly as HMC.

It is a best case, not a general result — which is exactly why it is worth including.

This is the most instructive number in the whole collection: the ranking of samplers can be reversed by a change of coordinates that leaves the problem itself untouched.

Summary

When to reach for it

Good when…

  • the model is conditionally conjugate, so conditionals are standard distributions;
  • there are discrete latent variables (mixtures, labels, changepoints);
  • there are very many parameters but weak coupling between blocks;
  • you want a sampler with no tuning knobs at all.

Bad when…

  • parameters are strongly correlated in the coordinates you have;
  • conditionals are awkward and every one needs its own sub-sampler;
  • the posterior has a funnel geometry (hierarchical models with small τ\tauτ);
  • you need the evidence ZZZ.

Next: Hamiltonian Monte Carlo moves along the ridge rather than across it, and nested sampling handles separated modes and computes ZZZ.

If you remember one thing: Gibbs is superb when the coordinates match the model's structure, and poor when they do not.
