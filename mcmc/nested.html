Nested sampling

Everything else here samples the posterior and cannot tell you the evidence. Nested sampling computes the evidence first, by squeezing an ensemble of live points into ever higher likelihood, and hands you posterior samples as a by-product. Because it works by level sets, separated modes do not stop it.

Nested sampling is the standard tool in cosmology and gravitational-wave astronomy, where model comparison matters as much as parameter estimation, and where posteriors are often multimodal.

The quantity nobody else computes

The evidence, and why it is hard

Bayes' theorem, written out in full:

P(θ∣D)⏟posterior=L(θ)⏞likelihood  π(θ)⏞priorZ⏟evidence,Z=∫L(θ) π(θ) dθ.\underbrace{P(\boldsymbol\theta\mid D)}_{\text{posterior}}= \frac{\overbrace{\mathcal L(\boldsymbol\theta)}^{\text{likelihood}}\;\overbrace{\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)}^{\text{prior}}}{\underbrace{Z}_{\text{evidence}}}, \qquad Z=\int \mathcal L(\boldsymbol\theta)\,\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)\,d\boldsymbol\theta .posteriorP(θ∣D)​​=evidenceZ​​L(θ)​likelihood​π(θ)​prior​​,Z=∫L(θ)π(θ)dθ.

MCMC never needs ZZZ, which is why it works at all. But ZZZ is exactly what you need to compare models:

P(M1∣D)P(M2∣D)=Z1Z2⏟Bayes factor×P(M1)P(M2).\frac{P(M_1\mid D)}{P(M_2\mid D)}=\underbrace{\frac{Z_1}{Z_2}}_{\text{Bayes factor}}\times\frac{P(M_1)}{P(M_2)} .P(M2​∣D)P(M1​∣D)​=Bayes factorZ2​Z1​​​​×P(M2​)P(M1​)​.

ZZZ is an average of the likelihood over the prior, and in many dimensions the posterior occupies a vanishing fraction of the prior volume, so naive Monte Carlo over the prior is hopeless: almost every draw contributes nothing.

Physics picture

ZZZ is a partition function, Z=∫e−UZ=\int e^{-U}Z=∫e−U, with −log⁡L-\log\mathcal L−logL playing the energy. Computing it is the free-energy problem, and the classical answers — thermodynamic integration, umbrella sampling — are all ways of bridging from a distribution you can sample to the one you want.

Skilling's idea (2004)

Sort the parameter space by likelihood instead of exploring it by position. The integral then collapses to one dimension, whatever ddd is.

Evidence differences of log⁡Z≳5\log Z\gtrsim5logZ≳5 are usually called decisive; the scale matters because the evidence penalises unused model flexibility automatically (the Occam factor).

The trick

Sort by likelihood: a one-dimensional integral

Define the prior volume enclosed above a likelihood level λ\lambdaλ:

X(λ)=∫L(θ)>λπ(θ) dθ  ∈[0,1].X(\lambda)=\int_{\mathcal L(\boldsymbol\theta)>\lambda}\pi(\boldsymbol\theta)\,d\boldsymbol\theta \;\in[0,1].X(λ)=∫L(θ)>λ​π(θ)dθ∈[0,1].

X=1X=1X=1 is the whole prior; X→0X\to0X→0 is the tiny region around the peak. Since XXX decreases monotonically with λ\lambdaλ, it can be inverted to give L(X)\mathcal L(X)L(X), and then

Z=∫L π dθ=∫01L(X) dX  ≈  ∑iLi ΔXi.Z=\int \mathcal L\,\pi\,d\boldsymbol\theta=\int_0^1 \mathcal L(X)\,dX \;\approx\;\sum_i \mathcal L_i\,\Delta X_i .Z=∫Lπdθ=∫01​L(X)dX≈i∑​Li​ΔXi​.

The ddd-dimensional integral has become the area under a one-dimensional curve. The problem is that we cannot compute XXX directly — but we can know its statistics.

The shrinkage law

Keep NNN points drawn from the prior restricted to L>Li\mathcal L>\mathcal L_iL>Li​. The largest of NNN uniform variables on (0,Xi)(0,X_i)(0,Xi​) has mean NN+1Xi\frac{N}{N+1}X_iN+1N​Xi​, so each time we discard the worst point the volume shrinks by a known random factor:

Xi+1Xi=ti,P(t)=NtN−1,E[log⁡t]=−1N.\frac{X_{i+1}}{X_i}=t_i,\quad P(t)=Nt^{N-1},\qquad \mathbb E[\log t]=-\frac1N .Xi​Xi+1​​=ti​,P(t)=NtN−1,E[logt]=−N1​.

Hence log⁡Xi≈−i/N\log X_i\approx-i/NlogXi​≈−i/N with standard deviation i/N\sqrt{i}/Ni​/N: the volume falls geometrically, and the uncertainty in log⁡Z\log ZlogZ is known in advance.

Why this is remarkable

The estimate of how much volume has been removed does not depend on the dimension, the shape of the likelihood, or how many modes there are. Only the sampling does.

This is the entire theoretical content of nested sampling: sorting by likelihood turns the evidence into a one-dimensional quadrature whose abscissae are known statistically.

The algorithm

Squeeze the live points upwards

  1. Draw NNN live points from the prior. Set X0=1X_0=1X0​=1, Z=0Z=0Z=0.
  2. Find the live point with the lowest likelihood Li\mathcal L_iLi​; record it as a dead point.
  3. Add its contribution: Z+=Li ΔXiZ\mathrel{+}=\mathcal L_i\,\Delta X_iZ+=Li​ΔXi​ with ΔXi=Xi−1−Xi\Delta X_i=X_{i-1}-X_iΔXi​=Xi−1​−Xi​ and Xi≈e−i/NX_i\approx e^{-i/N}Xi​≈e−i/N.
  4. Replace it with a new point drawn from the prior subject to L>Li\mathcal L>\mathcal L_iL>Li​ — the one hard step.
  5. Repeat until the remaining live points can add no more than a tolerance (Lmax⁡Xi<ϵZ)\big(\mathcal L_{\max}X_i<\epsilon Z\big)(Lmax​Xi​<ϵZ); then add the final ensemble.

Posterior samples come free: each dead point carries weight

wi=Li ΔXiZ,w_i=\frac{\mathcal L_i\,\Delta X_i}{Z},wi​=ZLi​ΔXi​​,

and resampling the dead points with these weights gives samples from the posterior.

Physics picture

An annealing schedule chosen by the algorithm itself: the constraint L>Li\mathcal L>\mathcal L_iL>Li​ plays the part of a slowly lowered temperature, but it is set by the data rather than guessed in advance. It passes through first-order phase transitions that defeat thermodynamic integration.

Error bars for free

The uncertainty in log⁡Z\log ZlogZ is dominated by the randomness of the volumes, giving σ(log⁡Z)≈H/N\sigma(\log Z)\approx\sqrt{\mathcal H/N}σ(logZ)≈H/N​ with H\mathcal HH the information (the Kullback–Leibler divergence from prior to posterior). More live points means a better answer, with cost growing as NNN.

Note how different this is from MCMC: there is no chain, no burn-in, no autocorrelation time. The output is a weighted set of points, and the run ends on a principled stopping criterion rather than a guess.

The hard step

Drawing a point above the level

Everything rests on step 4: a fresh, independent draw from the prior restricted to L>Li\mathcal L>\mathcal L_iL>Li​. Rejection sampling from the prior works at first and becomes hopeless, since the acceptance rate is Xi≈e−i/NX_i\approx e^{-i/N}Xi​≈e−i/N. The practical schemes are:

  • Ellipsoidal decomposition (MultiNest): bound the live points with one or several ellipsoids and sample inside them. Fast in low dimension; the bound becomes unreliable above roughly 20–30 parameters.
  • Slice sampling in a whitened space (PolyChord): start from a random live point and take a few slice-sampling steps in coordinates whitened by the live-point covariance. Cost grows like ddd rather than exponentially, so it scales to hundreds of parameters.
  • Diffusive and dynamic variants: dynamic nested sampling (dynesty) spends extra live points where they reduce the posterior or evidence error most.
The failure mode

If the new point is not independent of the one it replaced — too few slice steps, or an ellipsoid that misses part of the region — the volume shrinks faster than e−1/Ne^{-1/N}e−1/N and ZZZ comes out biased, usually too small. Unlike a badly mixed chain, this failure is quiet.

The fifth sampler in the tests

The nested sampler used in the comparison is PolyChord-style: constrained draws by repeated one-dimensional slice sampling in a whitened space. Cluster identification, which PolyChord adds, is not implemented, and that is the part that would sharpen the per-mode weights most on a four-mode problem.

A useful check: run with two very different numbers of live points, or with more slice steps per draw, and confirm that log⁡Z\log ZlogZ is stable within its quoted error.

Why it works here

Nested sampling explores a level set, not a neighbourhood. While the likelihood level is below the saddle between the peaks, the live points roam freely; as the level rises the ensemble simply splits, keeping points in every peak in proportion to its volume. No barrier is ever crossed, because the constraint region is disconnected by then.

Gibbs beat it only because that particular mixture is axis-aligned, its best case. Nested sampling needed no such luck, and also returned log⁡Z\log ZlogZ.

3,158 dead points were used, at about 38 likelihood calls per replacement. It is not cheap, but it is the only entry in the table that is robust to multimodality without being told the structure in advance.

Costs and limits

What you pay for the evidence

Number of iterations

To reach the posterior bulk needs ∼NH\sim N\mathcal H∼NH iterations, where H\mathcal HH is the information gained from prior to posterior (typically a few tens of nats). Each iteration needs one constrained draw, itself tens of likelihood calls.

Accuracy

σ(log⁡Z)≈H/N\sigma(\log Z)\approx\sqrt{\mathcal H/N}σ(logZ)≈H/N​: to halve the error, quadruple the live points. Posterior moments are usually less accurate than a good MCMC run of the same cost.

Dimension

Ellipsoidal methods degrade above roughly 20–30 parameters; slice-based ones scale roughly linearly in ddd per draw, and have been used for hundreds. Beyond that, gradient-based samplers win for parameter estimation.

Rules of thumb

Use NNN of a few hundred for a first look, a few thousand for a published evidence. Always repeat with a different seed. Quote log⁡Z\log ZlogZ with its error, and remember it depends on the prior: widening a prior lowers the evidence, which is the Occam penalty doing its job, not a bug.

If you only want parameters, and the posterior is unimodal and smooth, NUTS will usually give better posteriors per unit of computer time. Use nested sampling when you need ZZZ, or when you distrust unimodality.

Summary

When to reach for it

Good when…

  • you need the evidence ZZZ for model comparison;
  • the posterior may be multimodal or oddly shaped;
  • you want a principled stopping rule and an error bar on the answer;
  • the prior is proper and genuinely encodes what you know.

Bad when…

  • you only want posterior samples and the posterior is smooth and unimodal;
  • the dimension is very large and no gradient-free scheme can draw efficiently;
  • the likelihood has narrow, hard-to-find peaks that the live points can miss entirely;
  • the prior is vague: the answer depends on it, honestly but sometimes inconveniently.

Contrast with NUTS, which is better at posteriors but cannot give you ZZZ; and with random-walk Metropolis, whose blindness to gradients is what let it stumble across barriers.

Software worth knowing: MultiNest, PolyChord, dynesty, UltraNest, nestle. All produce weighted dead points plus log⁡Z\log ZlogZ and its error.
