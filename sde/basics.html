Brownian motion, and
why calculus breaks

A stochastic differential equation looks like an ordinary one with a noise term bolted on. It is not. The noise is so rough that the chain rule acquires an extra term, and the integral acquires an ambiguity that changes the physics.

The process

The Wiener process, in four properties

Standard Brownian motion W(t)W(t)W(t) is defined by four statements:

  • W(0)=0W(0)=0W(0)=0;
  • independent increments — W(t2)−W(t1)W(t_2)-W(t_1)W(t2​)−W(t1​) is independent of anything before t1t_1t1​;
  • stationary Gaussian increments — W(t+τ)−W(t)∼N(0,τ)W(t+\tau)-W(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\tau)W(t+τ)−W(t)∼N(0,τ);
  • continuous paths.

The third is the one to stare at. The variance grows linearly, so the increment itself scales as

ΔW∼Δt .\Delta W\sim\sqrt{\Delta t}\,.ΔW∼Δt​.

That single line is why stochastic calculus is different. For a smooth function Δf∼Δt\Delta f\sim\Delta tΔf∼Δt, so (Δf)2∼(Δt)2(\Delta f)^2\sim(\Delta t)^2(Δf)2∼(Δt)2 is negligible and second-order terms drop out of the chain rule. For Brownian motion (ΔW)2∼Δt(\Delta W)^2\sim\Delta t(ΔW)2∼Δt is first order, and it survives.

The same scaling says ΔW/Δt∼(Δt)−1/2→∞\Delta W/\Delta t\sim(\Delta t)^{-1/2}\to\inftyΔW/Δt∼(Δt)−1/2→∞: Brownian paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable. "White noise" ξ=dW/dt\xi=dW/dtξ=dW/dt does not exist as a function. It is a convenient fiction, and the honest object is the increment dWdWdW.

Physics picture

Einstein 1905: a pollen grain kicked by 102110^{21}1021 molecular collisions a second. Each kick is tiny and independent, so the central limit theorem gives Gaussian increments, and the number of kicks scales with elapsed time, so the variance does too. ⟨x2⟩=2Dt\langle x^2\rangle = 2Dt⟨x2⟩=2Dt is the third property.

Why not just write ξ(t)\xi(t)ξ(t)?

Because ⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=δ(t−t′)\langle\xi(t)\xi(t')\rangle=\delta(t-t')⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=δ(t−t′) means ξ\xiξ has infinite variance at every instant. Physicists use it happily and get the right answers, because the delta function is always integrated eventually. Writing dWdWdW instead simply does the integration first.

The scalings to keep

  E[dW]=0\;\mathbb{E}[dW]=0E[dW]=0,   E[dW2]=dt\;\mathbb{E}[dW^2]=dtE[dW2]=dt,   dW dt→0\;dW\,dt\to0dWdt→0,   dt2→0\;dt^2\to0dt2→0.
Everything on the next page is bookkeeping with those four rules.

The non-differentiability is not a technicality to be waved away — it is precisely why the ordinary chain rule fails, and why two reasonable definitions of the integral give different answers.

The key fact

dW2=dtdW^2=dtdW2=dt, and why it is not sloppy

The statement is about quadratic variation. Partition [0,T][0,T][0,T] into nnn pieces and form

Qn=∑k=1n(W(tk)−W(tk−1))2.Q_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(W(t_k)-W(t_{k-1})\big)^2 .Qn​=k=1∑n​(W(tk​)−W(tk−1​))2.

Each term has mean Δt\Delta tΔt and variance 2(Δt)22(\Delta t)^22(Δt)2, and they are independent, so

E[Qn]=n Δt=T,Var⁡(Qn)=2n(Δt)2=2T2n→n→∞0.\mathbb{E}[Q_n]=n\,\Delta t=T,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Q_n)=2n(\Delta t)^2=\frac{2T^2}{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0 .E[Qn​]=nΔt=T,Var(Qn​)=2n(Δt)2=n2T2​n→∞​0.

So Qn→TQ_n\to TQn​→T with probability one — not on average, but for every realisation. The sum of squared increments is deterministic even though every increment is random: a theorem, not an abuse of notation. For a smooth path ∑(Δf)2∼n(Δt)2→0\sum(\Delta f)^2\sim n(\Delta t)^2\to0∑(Δf)2∼n(Δt)2→0, so quadratic variation is exactly what separates Brownian motion from anything differentiable.

The consequence, in advance

Expand f(W+dW)f(W+dW)f(W+dW) to second order:

df=f′ dW+12f′′ dW2=f′ dW+12f′′ dt.df=f'\,dW+\tfrac12 f''\,dW^2=f'\,dW+\tfrac12 f''\,dt .df=f′dW+21​f′′dW2=f′dW+21​f′′dt.

The last term is not present in ordinary calculus and does not vanish. It is the whole of Ito's lemma, and it is why E[f(W)]\mathbb{E}[f(W)]E[f(W)] drifts even though E[W]=0\mathbb{E}[W]=0E[W]=0.

Physics picture

A diffusing particle explores more of a potential than a drifting one, so curvature of the potential biases its average energy upward. The 12f′′dt\tfrac12 f''dt21​f′′dt term is that bias. It is also the Laplacian in the diffusion equation, arriving from the other direction.

The equation

An SDE is an integral equation

The differential form

dXt=a(Xt,t) dt+b(Xt,t) dWtdX_t=a(X_t,t)\,dt+b(X_t,t)\,dW_tdXt​=a(Xt​,t)dt+b(Xt​,t)dWt​

is shorthand: dW/dtdW/dtdW/dt does not exist. What is meant is

XT=X0+∫0Ta(Xt,t) dt+∫0Tb(Xt,t) dWt,X_T=X_0+\int_0^T a(X_t,t)\,dt+\int_0^T b(X_t,t)\,dW_t ,XT​=X0​+∫0T​a(Xt​,t)dt+∫0T​b(Xt​,t)dWt​,

and the second integral is the problem. Define it as a limit of sums,

∫0Tb dW=lim⁡n→∞∑kb(Xτk)(Wtk+1−Wtk),τk=(1−α)tk+αtk+1,\int_0^T b\,dW=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_k b\big(X_{\tau_k}\big)\big(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_k}\big), \qquad \tau_k=(1-\alpha)t_k+\alpha t_{k+1},∫0T​bdW=n→∞lim​k∑​b(Xτk​​)(Wtk+1​​−Wtk​​),τk​=(1−α)tk​+αtk+1​,

and the answer depends on α\alphaα. For a smooth integrand it would not. Here the increments are of size Δt\sqrt{\Delta t}Δt​, the correlation between b(Xτk)b(X_{\tau_k})b(Xτk​​) and ΔWk\Delta W_kΔWk​ is of order αΔt\alpha\sqrt{\Delta t}αΔt​, and the product accumulates to something finite.

  • α=0\alpha=0α=0, left endpoint: the Ito integral.
  • α=12\alpha=\tfrac12α=21​, midpoint: the Stratonovich integral, written b∘dWb\circ dWb∘dW.
This is not a convention like a sign

The two give different answers for the same written equation — measured on page 2 at a 65% difference. Writing down an SDE without saying which integral you mean is like writing an integral without saying which variable.

Why each

Ito looks only backwards, so ∫b dW\int b\,dW∫bdW is a martingale with zero mean — clean mathematics, and why finance uses it. Stratonovich obeys the ordinary chain rule and is the limit of real noise with a short correlation time.

Wong-Zakai

Replace white noise by smooth noise of correlation time τ\tauτ, solve with ordinary calculus, let τ→0\tau\to0τ→0: the limit is the Stratonovich solution. Physically real noise is Stratonovich.

When the noise is additive — bbb constant — the two calculi agree, which is why the distinction never arises for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process and why many physicists never meet it.

Two standard processes

The two you will meet constantly

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck — the physicist's SDE
dX=−θ(X−m) dt+σ dWdX=-\theta(X-m)\,dt+\sigma\,dWdX=−θ(X−m)dt+σdW

A particle in a harmonic trap with thermal kicks. Additive noise, so Ito and Stratonovich agree. Mean reverts exponentially with time constant 1/θ1/\theta1/θ; the stationary distribution is Gaussian with

Var⁡∞=σ22θ.\operatorname{Var}_\infty=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}.Var∞​=2θσ2​.

That is the fluctuation-dissipation relation in its simplest form: the strength of the kicks divided by the strength of the damping. Measured in the demonstrations on page 4 to within 2%.

Geometric Brownian motion — where the trouble shows
dX=μX dt+σX dWdX=\mu X\,dt+\sigma X\,dWdX=μXdt+σXdW

Multiplicative noise, so the two calculi disagree, and the exact solutions differ:

XIto=X0e(μ−σ2/2)t+σW,XStrat=X0eμt+σW.X^{\rm Ito}=X_0e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)t+\sigma W},\qquad X^{\rm Strat}=X_0e^{\mu t+\sigma W}.XIto=X0​e(μ−σ2/2)t+σW,XStrat=X0​eμt+σW.

The −σ2/2-\sigma^2/2−σ2/2 is the Ito correction, and it is not small: at σ=1\sigma=1σ=1, t=1t=1t=1 it is a factor of 1.651.651.65. Page 2 measures both.

Where multiplicative noise comes from

Whenever the noise strength depends on the state: population dynamics, fluctuating growth rates, parametric driving, intensity noise in a laser. If your noise amplitude contains XXX, you must state which calculus you mean.

Nearly every SDE in a physics paper is one of these two, or a multi-dimensional version of one of them.
