Physics picture

A particle jiggling in a curved potential samples both sides. If the landscape curves upward, the average of fff over the jiggle exceeds fff at the mean position — Jensen's inequality, made dynamical. The correction term is that excess per unit time.

In Stratonovich

There is no correction: df=fx∘dXdf=f_x\circ dXdf=fx​∘dX exactly as in ordinary calculus. That convenience is the chief argument for Stratonovich, and it is paid for by losing the zero-mean property of the integral.

Every stochastic-calculus manipulation you will ever do is this expansion plus the four rules from page 1.

The means are equally instructive. Under Ito, E[Xt]=X0eμt\mathbb{E}[X_t]=X_0e^{\mu t}E[Xt​]=X0​eμt exactly, because the Ito integral has zero mean. That is not a paradox; it is what a lognormal distribution does, and it is why medians and means part company in this subject.

Conversion

The Stratonovich equation dX=a~ dt+b∘dWdX=\tilde a\,dt+b\circ dWdX=a~dt+b∘dW is the Ito equation

dX=(a~+12b ∂xb)dt+b dW.dX=\Big(\tilde a+\tfrac12 b\,\partial_x b\Big)dt+b\,dW .dX=(a~+21​b∂x​b)dt+bdW.

For GBM, 12b b′=12σ2X\tfrac12b\,b'=\tfrac12\sigma^2X21​bb′=21​σ2X, which is exactly the gap. When bbb is constant the correction vanishes and the two calculi coincide.

Which should you use?

If the noise is an idealisation of something with a short correlation time — thermal, turbulent, most physics — Stratonovich. If it is genuinely a limit of discrete independent shocks decided before the step — Ito. State which, always.

The constant-mean-but-decaying-paths behaviour of driftless GBM is worth showing people once; it dismantles the instinct that the mean describes a typical path.

The practical rule

Decide once, write it in the paper, and convert explicitly with 12bb′\tfrac12 bb'21​bb′ if you need the other. Most confusion in the literature comes from authors who never stated which they meant and readers who assumed the other.
