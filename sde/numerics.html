The schemes

Euler-Maruyama, and what it misses

The obvious scheme replaces dtdtdt by Δt\Delta tΔt and dWdWdW by a Gaussian draw of variance Δt\Delta tΔt:

Xn+1=Xn+a(Xn)Δt+b(Xn) ΔWn,ΔWn∼N(0,Δt).X_{n+1}=X_n+a(X_n)\Delta t+b(X_n)\,\Delta W_n,\qquad \Delta W_n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Delta t).Xn+1​=Xn​+a(Xn​)Δt+b(Xn​)ΔWn​,ΔWn​∼N(0,Δt).

The essential detail is ΔW∼Δt\Delta W\sim\sqrt{\Delta t}ΔW∼Δt​, not Δt\Delta tΔt: halving the step shrinks the noise term only by 2\sqrt22​. An Ito-Taylor expansion shows a term of order Δt\Delta tΔt has been dropped; restoring it gives Milstein:

Xn+1=Xn+aΔt+b ΔW+12 b b′ (ΔW2−Δt).X_{n+1}=X_n+a\Delta t+b\,\Delta W+\tfrac12\,b\,b'\,\big(\Delta W^2-\Delta t\big).Xn+1​=Xn​+aΔt+bΔW+21​bb′(ΔW2−Δt).

ΔW2−Δt\Delta W^2-\Delta tΔW2−Δt has mean zero: it is the fluctuation of quadratic variation about its deterministic limit. When b′=0b'=0b′=0 — additive noise — the correction vanishes and Milstein is Euler-Maruyama, so every Ornstein-Uhlenbeck run you have done was already first order.

The scheme picks the calculus

Euler-Maruyama converges to the Ito solution. A midpoint or Heun scheme converges to the Stratonovich one. Same equation on paper, different answers on screen — as page 2 measured at a ratio of 2.71.

To integrate a Stratonovich equation with an Ito scheme, convert first: add 12bb′\tfrac12bb'21​bb′ to the drift.

Do not reuse the increment

ΔW\Delta WΔW must be drawn fresh each step and must have variance Δt\Delta tΔt exactly. Using Δt\sqrt{\Delta t}Δt​ times a uniform, or recycling draws across a coarse and fine run, quietly destroys the convergence order you are trying to measure.

Higher-order schemes exist (stochastic Runge-Kutta, order 1.5 Taylor) but need multiple stochastic integrals per step and are rarely worth it in one dimension.

Two convergences

Pathwise or distributional

There are two reasonable questions, and they have different answers.

Strong convergence — does the computed path follow the true path driven by the same noise?

E∣XN−X(T)∣≤C Δtγ.\mathbb{E}\big|X_N-X(T)\big|\le C\,\Delta t^{\gamma}.E​XN​−X(T)​≤CΔtγ.

Milstein buys a factor of two in strong order and nothing in weak order. So if you only want averages — a mean, a variance, an option price, a rate constant — Euler-Maruyama is already as good, and the extra term is wasted effort.

Which do you need?

Strong: when individual trajectories matter — pathwise sensitivities, multilevel Monte Carlo, comparing to a measured time series, any case where you condition on a realisation.

Weak: when only expectations matter, which in statistical physics is most of the time.

Physics picture

Strong convergence asks whether this molecule followed the right trajectory; weak convergence asks whether the gas has the right temperature. The second is nearly always the question, and it is cheaper.

In practice

Stability, stiffness, and step size

Stability

Explicit schemes inherit the deterministic limit: for dX=−θX dt+σdWdX=-\theta X\,dt+\sigma dWdX=−θXdt+σdW, Euler-Maruyama needs θΔt<2\theta\Delta t<2θΔt<2 or the mean diverges — and the noise disguises it as a slow drift for a while. Diagnosis: halve Δt\Delta tΔt.

Stiffness

Separated timescales force Δt\Delta tΔt down to the fastest even when you care about the slowest. The cures are the deterministic ones — implicit, semi-implicit, exponential integrators — but keep the noise term explicit, since treating dWdWdW implicitly changes the calculus.

Choosing Δt\Delta tΔt

Halve the step and confirm nothing changes; then confirm a known quantity is reproduced — the stationary variance σ2/2θ\sigma^2/2\thetaσ2/2θ, say. Statistical error falls as 1/N1/\sqrt N1/N​ and discretisation error as Δtβ\Delta t^\betaΔtβ: balance them.

The exact Ornstein-Uhlenbeck update

Because OU is linear with additive noise, the transition density is Gaussian and can be sampled exactly at any step size:

Xn+1=m+(Xn−m)e−θΔt+σ22θ(1−e−2θΔt)  ξn,ξn∼N(0,1).X_{n+1}=m+(X_n-m)e^{-\theta\Delta t}+\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}\big(1-e^{-2\theta\Delta t}\big)}\;\xi_n, \qquad \xi_n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).Xn+1​=m+(Xn​−m)e−θΔt+2θσ2​(1−e−2θΔt)​ξn​,ξn​∼N(0,1).

No discretisation error, at any Δt\Delta tΔt. Integrate linear parts exactly and treat only the rest numerically — the basis of exponential integrators.

The exact OU update is worth knowing by heart; it turns up in Langevin thermostats, ancestral sampling in diffusion models, and Gaussian-process time series alike.

Summary

Six things to remember

  1. ΔW∼Δt\Delta W\sim\sqrt{\Delta t}ΔW∼Δt​: halving the step buys 2\sqrt22​, not 2.
  2. Euler-Maruyama is strong order 1/2, weak order 1.
  3. Milstein is strong order 1 and weak order 1 — no weak gain.
  4. With additive noise they are the same scheme.
  5. The scheme chooses the calculus; convert explicitly if you need the other.
  6. Integrate linear parts exactly whenever you can.
