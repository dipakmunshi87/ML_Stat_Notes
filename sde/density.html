Physics picture

This is the diffusion equation with a drift, and for Langevin dynamics it is the Smoluchowski equation. With a=0a=0a=0 and bbb constant it reduces to ∂tp=D∂x2p\partial_tp=D\partial_x^2p∂t​p=D∂x2​p with D=b2/2D=b^2/2D=b2/2 — Einstein's relation, with the factor of two that catches everyone.

The Stratonovich form differs

For multiplicative noise the Stratonovich reading gives 12∂x(b ∂x(bp))\tfrac12\partial_x\big(b\,\partial_x(bp)\big)21​∂x​(b∂x​(bp)) instead. The two agree only when bbb is constant. This is the commonest place the calculus choice silently changes a published result.

Two descriptions, one process

The SDE follows one realisation; Fokker-Planck follows the whole ensemble. Neither contains more information. Simulate when the dimension is high or the geometry awkward; solve the PDE when the dimension is low and you want the tails accurately.

The integration-by-parts derivation is worth doing once. It makes clear that Fokker-Planck is simply the generator's adjoint.

Equilibrium

Stationary solutions and Boltzmann

Set ∂tp=0\partial_tp=0∂t​p=0. In one dimension with reflecting or decaying boundaries the current must vanish identically — detailed balance — giving a first-order equation that integrates directly:

Now take overdamped Langevin dynamics in a potential UUU at temperature TTT:

The Boltzmann distribution, out of a stochastic differential equation. The relation between the noise amplitude 2T\sqrt{2T}2T​ and the damping is exactly what makes the stationary state thermal; change either without the other and the system equilibrates to the wrong temperature.

Fluctuation-dissipation

When detailed balance fails

Above one dimension a stationary state need not have zero current. Non-equilibrium steady states carry persistent currents — motors, active matter, driven systems — and p∞p_\inftyp∞​ is then neither Boltzmann nor closed-form.

The step from "SDE" to "Boltzmann" is the one that makes this subject feel like physics rather than probability theory.
