The one fact everything follows from

Brownian increments scale as ΔW∼Δt\Delta W\sim\sqrt{\Delta t}ΔW∼Δt​, so their squares are first order in Δt\Delta tΔt rather than negligible. Summed over a partition they converge to something deterministic:

The integral acquires an ambiguity, because where you stand inside each interval now matters. The Fokker-Planck equation acquires its second derivative. Physics picture

Einstein's 1905 pollen grain: 102110^{21}1021 independent collisions a second, each negligible, summing by the central limit theorem to a Gaussian whose variance grows with elapsed time. ⟨x2⟩=2Dt\langle x^2\rangle=2Dt⟨x2⟩=2Dt is the defining property, and the rest is bookkeeping.

The sentence to remember

An SDE written without stating Ito or Stratonovich is an incomplete statement. On page 2 the two readings of the same written equation differ by a measured factor of 2.71.

Four results from these pages

The calculus is not a convention

The same written equation dX=X dWdX=X\,dWdX=XdW, integrated by Euler-Maruyama and by Heun from the identical Brownian path, gives means differing by a factor of 2.71 — against a theoretical eσ2T/2=2.72e^{\sigma^2T/2}=2.72eσ2T/2=2.72. Each scheme agrees with its own exact solution to four digits, so the gap is the mathematics, not the numerics.

Half-integer convergence is real

Euler-Maruyama's strong order is measured at 0.51 and Milstein's at 0.94, against 0.5 and 1.0. Halving the step buys 2\sqrt22​, not 2 — and Milstein buys nothing at all if you only want averages, because both have weak order 1.

Fluctuation-dissipation, measured

An ensemble of 20,000 Ornstein-Uhlenbeck trajectories settles at variance 0.5006 against the predicted σ2/2θ=0.5000\sigma^2/2\theta=0.5000σ2/2θ=0.5000. The kicks that drive the system and the damping that calms it are the same physics, and equilibrium is where they balance.

Quantum mechanics from random walks

Weighting Brownian paths by e−∫Ve^{-\int V}e−∫V and fitting the decay of log⁡Z(T)\log Z(T)logZ(T) gives the harmonic oscillator's ground-state energy as 0.503 against exactly 0.50.50.5, and the weighted endpoints trace ψ0∝e−x2/2\psi_0\propto e^{-x^2/2}ψ0​∝e−x2/2. No Schrodinger equation is solved anywhere.

Five habits worth having

Do

  1. State Ito or Stratonovich, every time, even when it does not matter.
  2. Fix the noise amplitude from fluctuation-dissipation, not by tuning.
  3. Halve the step and confirm nothing changes, before anything else.
  4. Reproduce a known quantity — a stationary variance, an analytic mean.
  5. Integrate linear parts of the drift exactly; the OU update is free.

Don't

  1. Assume your library's default calculus matches your equation's.
  2. Use Milstein when you only want expectations — it buys nothing weakly.
  3. Read a small statistical error as a small total error; discretisation bias does not average away.
  4. Expect the mean to describe a typical path — lognormals are not symmetric.
  5. Simulate rare barrier crossings directly; the waiting time is exponential.
