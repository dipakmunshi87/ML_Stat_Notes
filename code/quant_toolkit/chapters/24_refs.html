24References and further reading

Textbooks first, in the order a reader of this note would want them; then the original papers behind each chapter.

Textbooks

  1. J. C. Hull, Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives, 11th ed., Pearson, 2021. The standard first book; covers Chapters 1, 9–13 at an introductory level.
  2. P. Wilmott, S. Howison and J. Dewynne, The Mathematics of Financial Derivatives, Cambridge University Press, 1995. The PDE view; the heat-equation transformation of Chapter 9 is from here.
  3. S. E. Shreve, Stochastic Calculus for Finance II: Continuous-Time Models, Springer, 2004. The rigorous version of Chapters 7–9 and 12.
  4. J. Gatheral, The Volatility Surface: A Practitioner's Guide, Wiley, 2006. Chapters 10–11: local and stochastic volatility, variance swaps, the smile.
  5. J.-P. Bouchaud and M. Potters, Theory of Financial Risk and Derivative Pricing: From Statistical Physics to Risk Management, 2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2003. The physicist's book: fat tails, random matrices, incomplete markets.
  6. R. Cont and P. Tankov, Financial Modelling with Jump Processes, Chapman & Hall, 2004. Chapter 11's jumps, Lévy processes and Fourier pricing.
  7. R. S. Tsay, Analysis of Financial Time Series, 3rd ed., Wiley, 2010. Chapters 14–15.
  8. J. Y. Campbell, A. W. Lo and A. C. MacKinlay, The Econometrics of Financial Markets, Princeton University Press, 1997. Chapters 3, 6, 14: the empirical finance canon.
  9. M. López de Prado, Advances in Financial Machine Learning, Wiley, 2018. Chapters 19–20: purged cross-validation, backtest overfitting, the deflated Sharpe ratio.
  10. R. C. Grinold and R. N. Kahn, Active Portfolio Management, 2nd ed., McGraw-Hill, 2000. Chapters 17 and 19: the fundamental law of active management, information coefficients.
  11. P. Glasserman, Monte Carlo Methods in Financial Engineering, Springer, 2004. Chapter 13: variance reduction, quasi-Monte Carlo, American options.
  12. A. J. McNeil, R. Frey and P. Embrechts, Quantitative Risk Management, 2nd ed., Princeton University Press, 2015. Chapter 18: coherent measures, copulas, extreme value theory.
  13. P. Embrechts, C. Klüppelberg and T. Mikosch, Modelling Extremal Events for Insurance and Finance, Springer, 1997. The EVT reference.
  14. D. Brigo and F. Mercurio, Interest Rate Models: Theory and Practice, 2nd ed., Springer, 2006. Chapter 12 in full.
