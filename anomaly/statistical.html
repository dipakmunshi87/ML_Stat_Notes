Part 1 of 5 · robust statistics

The estimator is computed
from the contaminated data

Every rule of the form “more than three standard deviations from the mean” has a circularity in it: the outliers you are hunting are inside the sum that sets the threshold. Past a certain contamination they raise the bar above themselves and the rule goes quietly blind.

The univariate rules

Four rules, and what each one is willing to assume

Start with the one everybody writes first. Flag xix_ixi​ when

Tukey's fences abandon the scale parameter entirely. With Q1Q_1Q1​ and Q3Q_3Q3​ the quartiles and IQR=Q3−Q1\mathrm{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1IQR=Q3​−Q1​, flag anything outside

Non-parametric, robust, and the right first look at any new variable. It is blind to correlation, which is the whole of the next page.

The formal tests

Grubbs' test, Dixon's Q and the generalised extreme studentised deviate test are hypothesis tests for a specified number of outliers, under normality. They give you a ppp-value, which nothing else here does. They are rarely used at scale, and they inherit the normality assumption completely.

Breakdown point, concretely

One observation sent to infinity takes the mean with it. To move the median you must corrupt half the sample. Everything robust on this page is a variation on that one substitution, and the price is efficiency: on genuinely clean Gaussian data the MAD is about 37%37\%37% as efficient as the standard deviation. You are buying insurance, and it is not free.

Two named failures

Masking — the outliers inflate the scale estimate and hide each other. A single gross outlier can conceal itself.

Swamping — a few extreme points distort the fit so badly that perfectly normal points get flagged. The false positives arrive alongside the false negatives, which is what makes the failure hard to spot from the alert count alone.

The efficiency figure is worth knowing: 2/π≈0.642/\pi \approx 0.642/π≈0.64 for the median relative to the mean, and about 0.370.370.37 for the MAD relative to the standard deviation, both at the Gaussian. Robustness is a trade, not a free improvement.

What that demonstration is really showing

The limit is the important part. The consequence for practice

A three-sigma rule is safe only when you already believe the contamination is below a few per cent — which is the thing you were trying to find out. Use the robust version by default. It costs one line.

Two dimensions

Mahalanobis distance, and the covariance the outliers wrote

Whitening turns an elliptical cloud into a spherical one, and distance in the whitened space is

d2(x)=(x−μ)⊤Σ−1(x−μ).d^2(x)=(x-\mu)^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu).d2(x)=(x−μ)⊤Σ−1(x−μ).

Under multivariate normality d2∼χp2d^2\sim\chi^2_pd2∼χp2​, so the threshold is principled rather than chosen: in two dimensions the 97.5%97.5\%97.5% point is d2=7.38d^2=7.38d2=7.38. This is the best-behaved method in the whole set when its assumption holds, and it is the only one that hands you a ppp-value.

The assumption that fails is not ellipticity. It is that μ\muμ and Σ\SigmaΣ were estimated from clean data. A compact clump of anomalies off the main axis rotates and inflates Σ^\hat\SigmaΣ^ until the confidence ellipse swallows it — and then reports that nothing is unusual.

Minimum covariance determinant is the repair. Search for the subset of h≈0.75nh\approx 0.75nh≈0.75n points whose covariance matrix has the smallest determinant, and estimate μ,Σ\mu,\Sigmaμ,Σ from that subset alone. Rousseeuw's insight is that the tightest three-quarters of the data cannot contain the outliers, so the estimate never sees them. In practice it is computed by concentration steps: fit, keep the hhh closest points, refit, repeat.

Scikit-learn ships it as EllipticEnvelope. It breaks down when ppp approaches nnn, or when the data is not elliptical at all.

What the determinant is doing

det⁡Σ\det\SigmadetΣ is the squared volume of the ellipse the data occupies. Minimising it over subsets is asking for the tightest possible packing of three-quarters of the points — a purely geometric criterion, with no distributional assumption used in the search itself.

Robust does not mean assumption-free

MCD still assumes the bulk is elliptical and unimodal. On the curved manifold of page 3 it fails as completely as the classical estimate does, and for a different reason.

The hhh in MCD is usually taken as ⌊(n+p+1)/2⌋\lfloor (n+p+1)/2 \rfloor⌊(n+p+1)/2⌋ for maximum breakdown, or around 0.75n0.75n0.75n for a better efficiency-robustness trade. The implementation on this page uses 0.75n0.75n0.75n with eight random starts and twelve concentration steps each.

The case that is not an error

Extreme value theory, for tails that are supposed to be there

Sometimes the extreme observation is a genuine draw from the true distribution: the largest flood in eighty years, the worst drawdown in a decade, the brightest transient in the survey. Deleting it is wrong, and flagging it as an anomaly answers the wrong question. What you want is a return period.

The peaks-over-threshold result says that for a high enough threshold uuu, the excesses X−u∣X>uX-u \mid X>uX−u∣X>u converge to a generalised Pareto distribution,

Pr⁡(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+ξyσ~)−1/ξ,\Pr(X-u>y\mid X>u)=\left(1+\frac{\xi y}{\tilde\sigma}\right)^{-1/\xi},Pr(X−u>y∣X>u)=(1+σ~ξy​)−1/ξ,

with the shape ξ\xiξ deciding everything that matters. ξ<0\xi<0ξ<0 gives a bounded tail; ξ=0\xi=0ξ=0 is the exponential limit; ξ>0\xi>0ξ>0 is heavy-tailed, and then moments start to fail to exist — the mean is infinite for ξ≥1\xi\ge 1ξ≥1, the variance for ξ≥1/2\xi\ge 1/2ξ≥1/2. Fit it to the exceedances and you can quote the level exceeded once per thousand observations, with an interval.

Mentioning this distinction unprompted is worth doing. The difference between “this point is anomalous” and “this point is a one-in-two-hundred-year event, as expected about now” is the difference between a bug report and a risk estimate.

The other route to the same place

Block maxima, fitted with the generalised extreme value distribution, is the older formulation and the one in the textbooks. Peaks-over-threshold uses the data more efficiently because it keeps every exceedance rather than one per block.

Choosing the threshold

The whole difficulty. Too low and the asymptotic result does not hold; too high and there is nothing left to fit. The standard diagnostics are the mean residual life plot and stability of the fitted ξ\xiξ across a range of uuu — if the shape parameter wanders as you move the threshold, the fit is not to be trusted.

Where this becomes an anomaly method again

A generalised Pareto fit to the tail of a score distribution is a principled way to set a detection threshold when you have no labels: choose the score exceeded once per NNN observations under the fitted tail. It is far better founded than picking the top 1%1\%1% because 1%1\%1% sounded reasonable.

Coles, An Introduction to Statistical Modeling of Extreme Values, is the standard reference and is unusually readable. The Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem is the result underneath the peaks-over-threshold approach.

Carried forward

What to take to the next page

Use robust estimators by default

Median and MAD instead of mean and standard deviation; MCD instead of the sample covariance. The cost is efficiency on clean data, which you can afford; the alternative is a rule that fails silently on dirty data, which you cannot.

Every method here assumes one shape

Unimodal and elliptical. When the data is two clusters of different density, or a curved manifold, none of this works — not because the estimators are fragile but because “centre plus spread” is not a description of the data.

The threshold is the deliverable

Mahalanobis with χ2\chi^2χ2 is the one method in the set that hands you a calibrated one. Almost everything on the following pages returns a score with no natural scale, and page 5 is about what to do then.
