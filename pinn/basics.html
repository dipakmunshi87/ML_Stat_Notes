Part 1 of 7 · the idea

A network that is the solution

Not a network trained on solutions. Not a network that interpolates data. A network whose output is the answer, found by making it fail to satisfy the differential equation as little as possible.

The move

Stop discretising. Parameterise.

Take any differential equation and write it as a residual that vanishes on the solution,

R[u](x)=0,x∈Ω,B[u]=0  on ∂Ω.\mathcal{R}[u](x)=0,\quad x\in\Omega,\qquad \mathcal{B}[u]=0\ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega.R[u](x)=0,x∈Ω,B[u]=0  on ∂Ω.

For a damped oscillator R[u]=u′′+2ζωu′+ω2u\mathcal{R}[u]=u''+2\zeta\omega u'+\omega^2uR[u]=u′′+2ζωu′+ω2u; for steady heat flow R[u]=−u′′−f\mathcal{R}[u]=-u''-fR[u]=−u′′−f.

Finite differences replace uuu by grid values and R\mathcal{R}R by a stencil; spectral methods expand uuu in basis functions. A PINN replaces uuu by a neural network and leaves R\mathcal{R}R as written:

u(x)  ⟶  uθ(x),θ⋆=arg⁡min⁡θ 1N∑i=1N∣R[uθ](xi)∣2.u(x)\;\longrightarrow\;u_\theta(x),\qquad \theta^\star=\arg\min_\theta\ \frac1{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big|\mathcal{R}[u_\theta](x_i)\big|^2 .u(x)⟶uθ​(x),θ⋆=argθmin​ N1​i=1∑N​​R[uθ​](xi​)​2.

The xix_ixi​ are collocation points — places where you check the equation. They need not form a grid, and can be resampled every step.

Physics picture

Rayleigh–Ritz with an unusual trial function: a family of candidates with adjustable parameters, varied to minimise a functional. Here the family is "everything this network can express" and the functional is the squared residual rather than an energy.

What is genuinely new

Not the variational principle — that is a century old. What is new is a trial family expressive enough to need no problem-specific design, whose derivatives are available exactly and cheaply in any dimension.

What you give up

Everything the classical methods guarantee: convergence order, stability analysis, evaluable error bounds — and a non-convex optimisation now stands between you and the answer.

The single sentence to take away: in a PINN the differential equation is the loss function and the solution is the trained network. Nothing is discretised except the set of points at which the equation is checked.

The trick

Derivatives that are exact

The residual needs u′u'u′ and u′′u''u′′. A finite-difference code gets them from neighbouring grid values and pays twice: truncation error O(h2)O(h^2)O(h2), and noise amplification ∼ε/h2\sim\varepsilon/h^2∼ε/h2 that makes small hhh counter-productive.

A network is an explicit composition of elementary functions, so its derivatives follow from the chain rule to machine precision. With ak=tanh⁡(zk)a_k=\tanh(z_k)ak​=tanh(zk​), zk=Wkak−1+bkz_k=W_ka_{k-1}+b_kzk​=Wk​ak−1​+bk​ and sk=1−ak2s_k=1-a_k^2sk​=1−ak2​ (which is tanh⁡′\tanh'tanh′ written through its own output), the input-derivatives propagate alongside the value:

ak′=sk zk′,ak′′=sk zk′′−2 ak sk (zk′)2.a_k'=s_k\,z_k',\qquad a_k''=s_k\,z_k''-2\,a_k\,s_k\,(z_k')^2 .ak′​=sk​zk′​,ak′′​=sk​zk′′​−2ak​sk​(zk′​)2.

That recursion is what the engine behind this page's demonstration evaluates. One extra pass per derivative order, and exact.

Two different differentiations

Forward, through the input: gives u′u'u′, u′′u''u′′ for the residual. Reverse, through the parameters: gives ∂L/∂θ\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\theta∂L/∂θ for the optimiser. A PINN needs both, nested — you differentiate a residual that already contains derivatives.

Why it matters in high dimension

A grid in ddd dimensions costs ndn^dnd points. Collocation points cost what you choose to spend. This is the one structural advantage no engineering will give a mesh-based method.

Not symbolic either

Automatic differentiation is neither computer algebra nor finite differences: it evaluates derivatives numerically and exactly, by applying the chain rule to the operations actually performed. No expression swell, no step size.

The a′′a''a′′ recursion above is worth checking by hand once. Differentiate a′=sz′a'=s z'a′=sz′ using s′=−2aa′=−2asz′s'=-2aa'=-2as z's′=−2aa′=−2asz′ and the second term appears immediately.

The loss

Physics, boundaries and data in one objective

The full loss is a weighted sum of what you know:

L(θ)=λrNr∑i∣R[uθ](xi)∣2⏟the equation+λbNb∑j∣B[uθ](xj)∣2⏟boundary / initial+λdNd∑k∣uθ(xk)−ukobs∣2⏟measurements, if any\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_r}{N_r}\sum_i\big|\mathcal{R}[u_\theta](x_i)\big|^2}_{\text{the equation}} +\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_b}{N_b}\sum_j\big|\mathcal{B}[u_\theta](x_j)\big|^2}_{\text{boundary / initial}} +\underbrace{\frac{\lambda_d}{N_d}\sum_k\big|u_\theta(x_k)-u_k^{\rm obs}\big|^2}_{\text{measurements, if any}}L(θ)=the equationNr​λr​​i∑​​R[uθ​](xi​)​2​​+boundary / initialNb​λb​​j∑​​B[uθ​](xj​)​2​​+measurements, if anyNd​λd​​k∑​​uθ​(xk​)−ukobs​​2​​

Three remarks, each a section later:

  • First two terms only: a solver. Add the third and it is an inverse problem — the same machinery, and the real selling point (page 5).
  • The weights λ\lambdaλ are not cosmetic: get them wrong and one term is satisfied at another's expense.
  • Boundary conditions need not be in the loss at all — build them into the architecture instead:
uθ(x)=g(x)+b(x) Nθ(x),b∣∂Ω=0.u_\theta(x)=g(x)+b(x)\,N_\theta(x),\qquad b\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0 .uθ​(x)=g(x)+b(x)Nθ​(x),b​∂Ω​=0.

Then B[uθ]=0\mathcal{B}[u_\theta]=0B[uθ​]=0 exactly, for every θ\thetaθ, and λb\lambda_bλb​ disappears. Page 3 shows the choice of bbb is worth a factor of fifty in accuracy.

Physics picture

Exactly the penalty-versus-essential-boundary-condition choice from finite elements. Soft constraints are penalties; hard constraints build the condition into the trial space. Same trade-offs, same conclusion.

Competing gradients

These terms disagree. Early on the residual term is enormous and the boundary term tiny, so the optimiser flattens the residual by drifting off the boundary values — and a wrong-but-smooth function can have a very small residual.

The honest description

A PINN does not solve the equation. It minimises a non-convex function whose global minimum would. The gap between those statements is where the subject lives.

The hard-constraint form is due to Lagaris, Likas and Fotiadis (1998) — a decade before deep learning, and still the right answer whenever the geometry allows it.

Worked example

A damped oscillator, end to end

Take the equation the demonstration solves:

u′′+2ζω u′+ω2u=0,u(0)=1,u′(0)=0,x∈[0,6],u''+2\zeta\omega\,u'+\omega^2u=0,\qquad u(0)=1,\quad u'(0)=0,\quad x\in[0,6],u′′+2ζωu′+ω2u=0,u(0)=1,u′(0)=0,x∈[0,6],

whose exact solution, for ζ<1\zeta<1ζ<1, is

u(x)=e−ζωx[cos⁡ωdx+ζωωdsin⁡ωdx],ωd=ω1−ζ2.u(x)=e^{-\zeta\omega x}\left[\cos\omega_d x+\frac{\zeta\omega}{\omega_d}\sin\omega_d x\right], \qquad \omega_d=\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}.u(x)=e−ζωx[cosωd​x+ωd​ζω​sinωd​x],ωd​=ω1−ζ2​.

Building the PINN takes four decisions:

  1. Architecture. Two hidden layers of 20 tanh⁡\tanhtanh units; 481 parameters. The input is rescaled to [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1] before it reaches the first layer.
  2. Constraints. Hard: uθ=1+b(x)Nθ(x)u_\theta=1+b(x)N_\theta(x)uθ​=1+b(x)Nθ​(x) with b=x2/(1+x2)b=x^2/(1+x^2)b=x2/(1+x2), so u(0)=1u(0)=1u(0)=1 and u′(0)=0u'(0)=0u′(0)=0 hold identically.
  3. Collocation. 120 points, evenly spaced. No grid is implied; they are sample locations.
  4. Optimiser. Adam, learning rate 5×10−35\times10^{-3}5×10−3.

The loss is then one term, 1N∑i∣uθ′′+2ζωuθ′+ω2uθ∣2\frac1N\sum_i|u_\theta''+2\zeta\omega u_\theta'+\omega^2u_\theta|^2N1​∑i​∣uθ′′​+2ζωuθ′​+ω2uθ​∣2, and there is nothing else to specify.

What "converged" means here

We can check honestly, because the exact solution is known. The figure of merit is the relative L2L^2L2 error ∥uθ−u∥2/∥u∥2\|u_\theta-u\|_2/\|u\|_2∥uθ​−u∥2​/∥u∥2​ over the whole interval — not the training loss, which can be small for a wrong answer.

Measured

With the settings above, 12000 Adam steps reach 0.06% at ω=2\omega=2ω=2 and 1.4% at ω=4\omega=4ω=4. The second number takes most of those steps to arrive; watch the demonstration and you will see why.

Note the cost

Thousands of optimiser steps to solve a linear second-order ODE that a Runge–Kutta integrator disposes of in microseconds. Nothing on this page is an argument for using a PINN here. It is an argument for understanding one.

Deliberately a problem with a known answer. Every claim about accuracy on these pages is measured against an exact solution or a converged classical solver, never against the training loss.

Summary

The vocabulary, once

TermWhat it means
residual R[u]\mathcal{R}[u]R[u]the equation written so it equals zero on the solution
collocation pointa place where the residual is evaluated; not a grid node

soft constrainta boundary condition added to the loss as a penalty
hard constrainta boundary condition built into the architecture, satisfied identically

inverse problemsome coefficients unknown, data available, solve for both
operator learninglearning the map from data to solution, not one solution
Three sentences worth keeping

The differential equation is the loss function.
The trained network is the solution.
Minimising the loss and solving the equation are not the same thing.
