The central correspondence

AR(1) is Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, sampled

The continuous Langevin equation for a particle in a harmonic trap, overdamped:

dxdt=−xτ+ξ(t),⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=2D δ(t−t′).\frac{dx}{dt}=-\frac{x}{\tau}+\xi(t),\qquad \langle\xi(t)\xi(t')\rangle=2D\,\delta(t-t') .dtdx​=−τx​+ξ(t),⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=2Dδ(t−t′).

Integrate it over one sampling interval Δt\Delta tΔt exactly:

xn+1=e−Δt/τ⏟φ xn+εn,Var⁡(ε)=Dτ(1−e−2Δt/τ).x_{n+1}=\underbrace{e^{-\Delta t/\tau}}_{\displaystyle\varphi}\,x_n+\varepsilon_n, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon)=D\tau\big(1-e^{-2\Delta t/\tau}\big).xn+1​=φe−Δt/τ​​xn​+εn​,Var(ε)=Dτ(1−e−2Δt/τ).

Where it turns up

Quasar and AGN light curves (the "damped random walk"), Brownian motion in a trap, velocity in a viscous fluid, temperature fluctuations with a thermostat, and the noise of an amplifier with a single dominant time constant.

Sampling matters

If Δt≫τ\Delta t\gg\tauΔt≫τ then φ→0\varphi\to0φ→0 and the process looks like white noise: the physics has been sampled away. If Δt≪τ\Delta t\ll\tauΔt≪τ then φ→1\varphi\to1φ→1 and it looks like a random walk. The same system gives completely different-looking data depending on cadence.

This slide is the one to remember. It is also a warning about interpreting a fitted φ without stating Δt: φ is not a property of the system alone.

Filters and responses

Green's functions with a different name

A linear, time-invariant system driven by noise is written in statistics as a moving average of the driving terms, and in physics as a convolution with a Green's function:

xt=∑k≥0hk εt−k⟺x(t)=∫0∞G(s) ξ(t−s) ds.x_t=\sum_{k\ge0} h_k\,\varepsilon_{t-k} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x(t)=\int_0^\infty G(s)\,\xi(t-s)\,ds .xt​=k≥0∑​hk​εt−k​⟺x(t)=∫0∞​G(s)ξ(t−s)ds.

Fourier transforming turns convolution into multiplication, giving the transfer function H(f)H(f)H(f) (the susceptibility χ(ω)\chi(\omega)χ(ω)), and the spectra obey

Sx(f)=∣H(f)∣2 Sε(f).S_x(f)=|H(f)|^2\,S_\varepsilon(f).Sx​(f)=∣H(f)∣2Sε​(f).

Everything follows: an AR model is an all-pole filter, an MA model an all-zero one, ARMA has both. Fitting an ARMA model to data is measuring a system's poles and zeros.

Causality and analyticity

hk=0h_k=0hk​=0 for k<0k<0k<0 — the future does not affect the past — is the statement that GGG is retarded, which makes H(f)H(f)H(f) analytic in the upper half plane and gives the Kramers–Kronig relations between its real and imaginary parts. The statisticians' "causal filter" is the physicists' retarded response.

Useful consequences
  • Whitening = applying the inverse filter 1/H1/H1/H; the result should be featureless.
  • Matched filtering = correlating with a template weighted by 1/S(f)1/S(f)1/S(f): the Wiener filter.
  • Deconvolution = dividing by H(f)H(f)H(f), with the familiar noise amplification where ∣H∣|H|∣H∣ is small.

The "innovation" in a statistics paper is the part of the new measurement not predicted by the model: in a Kalman filter it is what drives the update, and if the model is correct it is white.

Where the language differs

Four places the two fields disagree

Normalisation of the spectrum

Statisticians usually use a one-sided PSD in units of variance per hertz; physicists often quote an amplitude spectral density (its square root) or use ω=2πf\omega=2\pi fω=2πf. Factors of 222, 2π2\pi2π and Δt\Delta tΔt differ between conventions. Always check against a known case, for example white noise of known variance.

"Noise" and "signal"

In statistics the stochastic part is often the object of interest; in physics it is usually the thing to be removed. Red noise in a detector is a nuisance; red noise in a quasar light curve is the measurement.

"Model"

An ARMA model makes no claim about mechanism: it is a compact description of the correlation structure. A physical model claims a mechanism. Both are called models, and conflating them causes trouble in referee reports.

Significance

A peak that is "3σ" against white noise may be entirely ordinary against the red noise the data actually have. Assess significance against the correct null process, not the convenient one.
