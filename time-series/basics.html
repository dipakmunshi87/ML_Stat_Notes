Part 1 of 4 · definitions

What is a time series?

A sequence of measurements in which the order matters. Shuffle a set of independent measurements and nothing is lost; shuffle a time series and you destroy the very thing you were trying to measure.

The object

Physics picture

This is an ensemble in the statistical-mechanics sense. The process is the ensemble; your data are one member of it. You are in the position of someone who must infer the properties of a whole ensemble from a single trajectory.

Why order matters

For independent measurements, the mean and its error follow from NNN and σ\sigmaσ. For a correlated series, the same formula can understate the error by a factor of ten or more. Ordering is information, and ignoring it is not conservative.

Vocabulary

Sampling interval Δt\Delta tΔt; length T=NΔtT=N\Delta tT=NΔt; cadence is the same as sampling rate; a series with gaps or irregular spacing is unevenly sampled, which changes which tools apply.

In astronomy the unevenly sampled case is the norm, not the exception: weather, scheduling and the Sun all conspire against a uniform cadence.

The two assumptions everything rests on

Stationarity and ergodicity

Stationarity

The statistics do not depend on when you look. Weak (or second-order) stationarity asks only that

E[x(t)]=μ  (constant),Cov⁡(x(t),x(t+τ))=γ(τ)  (depends only on τ).\mathbb E[x(t)]=\mu\ \ \text{(constant)},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}\big(x(t),x(t+\tau)\big)=\gamma(\tau)\ \ \text{(depends only on }\tau).E[x(t)]=μ  (constant),Cov(x(t),x(t+τ))=γ(τ)  (depends only on τ).

Strict stationarity asks the same of the whole joint distribution. Weak stationarity is what estimators actually use.

Ergodicity

Time averages over one realisation converge to ensemble averages:

1T∫0Tx(t) dt  →T→∞  E[x].\frac1T\int_0^T x(t)\,dt\;\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{}\;\mathbb E[x] .T1​∫0T​x(t)dtT→∞​E[x].

Without it, a single record tells you nothing about the ensemble, however long it is.

Physics picture

Exactly the assumptions of equilibrium statistical mechanics. Stationarity is equilibrium: the system's statistics do not drift. Ergodicity is Boltzmann's hypothesis that a single long trajectory explores the ensemble. Both can fail in the same ways: ageing, glassy dynamics, broken symmetry.

When stationarity fails

Instrumental drift, a transient, a changing source, a changing background. Three standard responses: detrend or difference; model the non-stationary part explicitly (a state-space model); or analyse in short windows and let the parameters vary (a spectrogram or wavelet analysis).

The practical test

Split the record in halves and compare mean, variance and spectrum. If they differ by more than their errors, treat the series as non-stationary and say so.

Differencing xt−xt−1x_t-x_{t-1}xt​−xt−1​ turns a random walk into white noise, which is exactly why the "I" appears in ARIMA. It is a high-pass filter and it also amplifies measurement noise, so it is not free.

Sampling

Nyquist, aliasing and leakage

Nyquist frequency

Sampling every Δt\Delta tΔt resolves frequencies only up to

fNyq=12Δt.f_{\rm Nyq}=\frac{1}{2\Delta t}.fNyq​=2Δt1​.

Below it the spectrum is recoverable; above it, it is not.

Aliasing

Power at f>fNyqf>f_{\rm Nyq}f>fNyq​ does not vanish: it is folded back to ∣f−2kfNyq∣|f-2kf_{\rm Nyq}|∣f−2kfNyq​∣ and masquerades as a low frequency. The cure is an analogue anti-alias filter before digitising; afterwards it is too late.

Leakage

A finite record multiplies the signal by a window, so the spectrum is convolved with that window's transform. A sharp line grows sidelobes falling only as 1/f21/f^21/f2. Tapering (Hann, Hamming, DPSS) trades resolution for much lower sidelobes.

Physics picture

Leakage is the window function of an interferometer or the beam pattern of a telescope: the same convolution theorem, with time and frequency in place of sky position and baseline. Aliasing is Brillouin-zone folding on a lattice.

Resolution

A record of length TTT resolves frequencies no finer than Δf≈1/T\Delta f\approx1/TΔf≈1/T. To separate two lines you need to observe for longer; averaging more samples at the same TTT will not do it.

In radio astronomy both effects are everyday: RFI above the band aliases into it, and a strong narrow-band transmitter leaks across many channels unless the window is chosen carefully.
