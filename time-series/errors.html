Errors · the part that decides whether a result is real

Error estimates, limitations and extensions

Almost every published time-series mistake is an error bar that is too small: correlations ignored, trials not counted, or significance judged against white noise when the data are red. This page collects the estimators, the traps and the standard ways out.

③ The power spectrum

A raw periodogram ordinate is not consistent: its distribution about the true value is χ22\chi^2_2χ22​, i.e. exponential, whatever NNN is.

④ Fitted parameters

From the likelihood: the curvature at the maximum (the Fisher matrix) for a quick estimate, or the full posterior by MCMC when the parameters are correlated or the likelihood is skewed — the samplers of the companion set do exactly this job.

The spectral result surprises people: more data does not reduce the error on a single periodogram ordinate, it only gives you more ordinates. Reduction of variance must be bought by averaging, and it costs frequency resolution.

The dominant one, in practice

Assuming white noise. If the true spectrum is red, a peak's local significance is computed against a level that is far too low. The fix is to fit the continuum (a power law, or an AR model) and assess peaks against that, with the fit's own uncertainty folded in.

Trials factor

Searching MMM independent frequencies, the chance that the largest of MMM exponential variables exceeds zzz times the mean is

P≈1−(1−e−z)M≈Me−z  (large z).P\approx 1-(1-e^{-z})^{M}\approx M e^{-z}\ \ (\text{large }z).P≈1−(1−e−z)M≈Me−z  (large z).

With M=104M=10^4M=104, a "3σ3\sigma3σ" local peak is entirely ordinary. Quote the global, trials-corrected significance, or a false-alarm probability calibrated by simulation.

Vaughan (2005, 2010) and Mann & Lees (1996) both give practical recipes for red-noise-aware significance; the Bayesian version fits the continuum and the line together.

When formulas run out

Resampling, surrogates and injections

Block bootstrap

Resampling individual points destroys the correlations. Instead resample blocks several correlation times long, so the dependence within a block is preserved. Block length is the tuning parameter; check that the answer is stable against it.

Surrogate data

Keep the measured spectrum but randomise the Fourier phases: this gives Gaussian series with the same correlations and no other structure. It is the standard null for tests of nonlinearity, determinism or periodicity (Theiler et al. 1992). Variants preserve the amplitude distribution as well.

Parametric bootstrap and injection

Simulate many series from the fitted model, run the entire pipeline on each, and look at the spread of the recovered quantity: this captures selection effects, filtering and search bias that no analytic formula covers. Injecting a known signal into real data is the same idea and calibrates both the bias and the error bar.

Model checking, in three tests

Whiteness: the residuals' autocorrelation should sit inside ±2/N\pm2/\sqrt N±2/N​, and Ljung–Box should not reject. Distribution: a quantile plot of the whitened residuals against a Gaussian. Predictive: simulate from the fitted model and compare any statistic you care about — the maximum, the variance of differences, the number of upcrossings — with the value in the data.

Posterior predictive checking is the Bayesian version of the third test and is worth doing even when the fit is by maximum likelihood.

Checklist

Before you quote a number

For a measured quantity

  1. Is the error bar computed with NeffN_{\rm eff}Neff​, not NNN?
  2. Was the record long compared with the correlation time? If not, say what that costs.
  3. Did any filtering or detrending change the quantity? Apply the same operations to simulations.
  4. Does a parametric bootstrap reproduce the quoted uncertainty?

For a claimed detection

  1. What is the null process — white, red, or the fitted continuum?
  2. How many frequencies, templates or trials were searched?
  3. Is the significance calibrated by simulation, or read from a formula that assumes Gaussian white noise?
  4. Does the result survive splitting the data in half?
  5. Would an injected signal of the claimed strength have been recovered with the claimed parameters?
