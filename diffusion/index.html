Diffusion and
score-based models

Destroy your data with noise on a schedule you control, learn one function — the gradient of the log density — and the destruction runs backwards. Generation becomes an initial-value problem you already know how to solve.

Eight pages, three demonstrations, each computing its numbers in your browser while you watch. Nothing here is a stored result.

The one equation everything follows from

Take any stochastic differential equation that carries your data towards noise:

dx=f(x,t) dt+g(t) dwdx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dwdx=f(x,t)dt+g(t)dw

Choose f and g so that the end state is something you can sample from directly — typically pT≈N(0,I)p_T \approx \mathcal{N}(0,I)pT​≈N(0,I). Anderson's 1982 result is that the same process run backwards in time is also a diffusion, and its equation differs by exactly one term:

dx=[ f(x,t)−g(t)2 ∇xlog⁡pt(x) ]dt+g(t) dwˉdx = \left[\,f(x,t) - g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x)\,\right]dt + g(t)\,d\bar{w}dx=[f(x,t)−g(t)2∇x​logpt​(x)]dt+g(t)dwˉ

Everything in this set is a consequence of that. The only object in the reverse equation you do not already know is ∇xlog⁡pt(x)\nabla_x \log p_t(x)∇x​logpt​(x), the score of the noised data at each time. Learn it and you can run the process backwards; the rest is numerical integration, which is a solved problem.

The reason this is tractable at all is that the forward process is chosen so each marginal has a closed form. For the variance-preserving choice,

xt=αˉt x0+1−αˉt  εx_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\;\varepsilonxt​=αˉt​​x0​+1−αˉt​​ε

so you can jump to any noise level in one step, without simulating a trajectory — which is what makes training cheap and the whole method practical.

Physics picture

This is the Fokker–Planck equation read in reverse. The forward process is an over-damped Langevin flow towards a Gaussian equilibrium; the reverse process is that flow undone, and the score plays the role of the force. If you have met Langevin dynamics as a sampler, you have met the reverse process already — the difference is that here the potential is learned rather than given.

The sentence to remember

A diffusion model is not learning to generate images. It is learning one vector field — the score — and generation is what you get when you integrate it.

What these pages will measure

The three demonstrations are built to answer three questions with numbers rather than assertion: does the learned score match the exact one, and where does it not; how many steps does each sampler actually need; and what a badly chosen noise schedule does to the result.

What you need before starting

Very little, but it is worth being honest about which parts matter.

  • Gaussians. Sums of them, conditionals of them, and the fact that the log of one has a linear gradient. That last fact is the entire reason denoising score matching works.
  • A little stochastic calculus. Enough to read an SDE and know that the noise term scales as the square root of the step. The stochastic differential equations set covers this properly; nothing here requires Itô calculus in anger.
  • Gradient descent. The training objective is an ordinary least-squares regression once you see what it is regressing onto.
